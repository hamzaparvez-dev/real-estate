How to Check a MahaRERA Registration Number: Step by Step

By Prestige Projects · Updated November 26, 2026 · 9 min read

Quick answer

To check a MahaRERA registration number, open the official MahaRERA website, go to the registered projects search, and enter the number or the project name. Then confirm that the promoter, project name, address, completion date and approved plans match what you were told, and read the quarterly progress updates. Never pay a booking amount for a project you cannot find on the portal.

Why the registration number matters

Under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, residential projects above a minimum size must be registered with MahaRERA, the Maharashtra regulator, before they are advertised, marketed or sold. Registration means the promoter has filed the project's land details, approvals, plans and a proposed completion date, and has agreed to keep buyers' money in a separate project account.

The registration number is your key to all of that information. With it, you can see what the promoter has declared to the regulator, rather than relying only on a brochure or an advert.

Where to find the number

Every advert for a registered project should show its MahaRERA registration number, and MahaRERA now also requires a QR code on project advertising that links to the project's page. You will also find the number on the project's website and in the draft agreement. For project numbers in Thane district, which includes Kalyan, the number usually begins with P517.

If the sales team cannot give you a registration number, or says it is "applied for", do not pay anything until the project appears on the portal.

  • On the advert, hoarding or brochure
  • On the project's website
  • Through the QR code on the advertisement
  • In the draft agreement for sale

Step by step: searching on the MahaRERA website

Go to the official MahaRERA website and open the section for registered projects. The layout of the website changes from time to time, but the search usually lets you look up a project by registration number, project name, promoter name or district.

Enter the registration number exactly as shown, including the letter P. If nothing appears, try the project name or the promoter name, and select Thane as the district. Open the project's page to see the full details.

  • Open the official MahaRERA website
  • Go to the registered projects search
  • Enter the registration number, or search by project or promoter name
  • Open the project page and download the documents listed there

What to read on the project page

The project page shows what the promoter has filed. Start with the basics: the promoter's name and address, the project name and its exact location, and the land or survey details. Check that these match the brochure and the name on the agreement you will sign.

Then look at the proposed completion date and whether it has been extended, the approved building plans and the commencement certificate, the number of units and parking declared, and the quarterly progress reports that show how construction has moved. Also look for any complaints filed against the project or promoter.

  • Promoter name and address
  • Project name, location and land details
  • Proposed completion date and any extensions
  • Approved plans and commencement certificate
  • Quarterly progress reports
  • Complaints against the project or promoter

Reading the quarterly progress reports

Registered promoters must update MahaRERA regularly with the progress of construction, usually every quarter. These updates typically show which stages of the building are complete, such as excavation, the plinth, each slab and the finishing work, along with information on the number of units booked and approvals received.

Compare the latest update with what you see on site. If the portal says the eighth slab is complete and you can see it, that is reassuring. If the updates have not changed for several quarters, ask the promoter why, and check whether the completion date has been extended.

  • Stages of construction completed
  • Units booked and remaining
  • Approvals and certificates received
  • Date of the latest update

Checking a real estate agent

Real estate agents who sell units in registered projects must also be registered with MahaRERA, and their registration numbers usually begin with the letter A. You can search for an agent on the same portal. Ask any agent or channel partner for their MahaRERA agent number and check that it is valid before you share documents or pay anything through them.

Payments for a new flat should always be made to the promoter's designated project account, as given in the agreement, not to an individual.

If something goes wrong

If a registered project is delayed or the promoter does not keep to the agreement, MahaRERA lets buyers file complaints online through the portal. MahaRERA also runs a conciliation forum that tries to settle disputes between buyers and promoters without a full hearing. Keep copies of your agreement, payment receipts, letters and emails, because the complaint will rely on them.

Before filing, write to the promoter setting out the problem and what you want; many issues are settled at this stage.

Matching the portal with what you were told

The point of the check is to compare. If the brochure says possession in 2027 but the portal shows a later completion date, the portal is the date the promoter has committed to the regulator. If the agreement names a different company from the registered promoter, ask why before you sign. If the number of floors or units in the brochure is higher than the approved plans, ask whether further approvals are pending.

Write down any differences and ask for answers in writing. A genuine promoter will be able to explain each one.

Red flags

Walk away, or at least pause, if any of these come up. They do not always mean fraud, but each is a reason to get answers before you pay.

  • No registration number, or a number "applied for"
  • A number that belongs to a different project or location
  • A promoter name on the agreement that differs from the portal
  • A possession date in the brochure earlier than the registered date
  • Quarterly updates that have stopped for a long time
  • Pressure to pay a booking amount in cash

What registration does and does not guarantee

Registration gives you important protections. Promoters must sell on RERA carpet area, keep a large share of buyers' money in a separate account for the project, and pay interest for delays as set out in the law. You also have a regulator to complain to if things go wrong.

But registration is a minimum check, not a certificate of quality. It does not tell you how well the building will be finished or how the developer has performed before. Combine it with a title check, a look at the developer's past projects and a careful reading of the agreement. Our documents to check before buying property checklist covers the rest.

Resale flats and RERA

If you are buying a resale flat in an older building, the project may never have been registered with MahaRERA, especially if it was completed before RERA came into force. In that case you rely on the title documents, the occupancy certificate and the society's records instead. Our resale flat checklist explains what to ask for.

Questions people ask

How do I check a MahaRERA registration number?
Open the official MahaRERA website, go to the registered projects search and enter the number, or search by project or promoter name.
What does a MahaRERA number look like?
It starts with the letter P followed by digits. Numbers for projects in Thane district, including Kalyan, usually begin with P517.
What should I check on the MahaRERA project page?
The promoter, project name and location, completion date and extensions, approved plans, quarterly progress reports and any complaints.
Is a RERA-registered project always safe?
Registration is an important minimum check, but not a guarantee of quality. Also check the title, approvals and the developer's track record.
Do resale flats have a RERA number?
Often not, especially in buildings completed before RERA. For resale, rely on the title, occupancy certificate and society records.
Can I check a real estate agent on MahaRERA?
Yes. Agents selling units in registered projects must be registered too, and their numbers usually begin with A. Search for the agent on the MahaRERA portal before you deal with them.
What if the completion date on MahaRERA is later than the brochure?
Treat the MahaRERA date as the one the promoter has committed to the regulator, and ask for the difference to be explained in writing before you sign.
