Total Cost of Buying a Flat in Kalyan West: Every Charge

By Prestige Projects · Updated November 26, 2026 · 10 min read

Quick answer

The total cost of a flat in Kalyan West is more than the agreement value. Depending on how the price is quoted, add GST for under-construction homes (usually 5%, or 1% for affordable homes, nil for ready homes with an occupancy certificate), stamp duty (commonly about 7% in Kalyan-Dombivli; confirm locally), registration (1%, capped at ₹30,000), plus legal, loan, society, maintenance, parking and interior costs. Always ask for a written cost sheet showing what is included.

Why the price on the advert is not the full cost

When you see a price for a flat in Kalyan West, it can mean different things. Some developers quote a base price, to which taxes and charges are added. Others, such as Prestige Gold, quote an all-in starting price. Resale flats are usually quoted as the amount the seller wants, with stamp duty, registration and brokerage paid on top by the buyer.

The only way to compare fairly is to ask every seller for a written cost sheet for the exact unit, listing each charge and saying clearly whether it is included or extra. This guide walks through every line you are likely to see.

1. Agreement value

The agreement value is the price written in your agreement for sale. It is the base for most of the other charges, including stamp duty and GST. Check that it matches the price you agreed, the RERA carpet area of your unit and any floor-rise or preferred-location charges you accepted.

Stamp duty is charged on the higher of the agreement value and the value worked out from the government's ready reckoner rate. Our guide to the ready reckoner rate in Kalyan explains how to check it.

2. GST on under-construction homes

GST applies only to homes bought before completion. For most under-construction homes, the rate is 5% without input tax credit. For homes that qualify as affordable housing, the rate is 1%: broadly, a value of up to ₹45 Lakh and a carpet area of up to 60 sq m in metropolitan cities or 90 sq m elsewhere. Ready-to-move homes that already have an occupancy certificate, and resale flats, do not attract GST.

Ask whether the price you have been quoted includes GST. On a ₹75 Lakh agreement value, 5% GST is ₹3,75,000, so it makes a large difference to the comparison.

  • Under construction: usually 5% (no input tax credit)
  • Affordable housing: 1%, if the value and carpet-area limits are met
  • Ready with occupancy certificate, or resale: no GST

3. Stamp duty

Stamp duty is a state tax paid when your agreement is registered. In Mumbai, the rate for residential property is 6%, including a 1% metro cess. In other municipal corporation areas such as Kalyan-Dombivli, a local body surcharge can also apply, and the total is commonly about 7%. Confirm the exact rate with the sub-registrar or on the IGR Maharashtra website before you budget.

Maharashtra gives women buying a residential property in their sole name a 1% concession, subject to conditions. Our guide to stamp duty and registration charges in Maharashtra explains the details.

4. Registration fee

The registration fee is 1% of the property value, capped at ₹30,000 for properties above ₹30 Lakh. For almost every new flat in Kalyan West, that means ₹30,000. You pay it when the agreement is registered at the sub-registrar's office, along with a small document handling charge.

5. TDS on purchases of ₹50 Lakh or more

If the price of the property is ₹50 Lakh or more, the buyer must deduct 1% TDS from the payments to the seller and deposit it with the income tax department, then give the seller the TDS certificate. This is not an extra cost, because it is part of the price, but it affects how you make each payment and must not be missed.

If you are buying from a non-resident seller, the TDS rules are different and higher; take professional advice.

6. Legal and documentation costs

Most buyers pay a lawyer to check the title and approvals, review the agreement and attend registration. Fees vary with the work involved. For resale flats, there may also be costs for searching old title records and for the society's no-objection certificate.

Our checklist of documents to check before buying property shows what the lawyer should look at.

7. Home loan charges

If you take a home loan, budget for the lender's processing fee, any legal and technical valuation charges, and the cost of registering the mortgage. Some lenders also ask for property or life insurance linked to the loan; you are not always required to buy it from them, so compare.

Remember that banks usually fund only part of the property value, and do not fund stamp duty or registration. Our home loan eligibility and EMI guide explains how much you can borrow.

8. Society, maintenance and parking

New projects commonly collect charges for forming the cooperative housing society, share money, and advance maintenance for a period after possession, often one to two years. Some also charge for development, club or amenity use, and for parking. Ask for each figure in writing and check what the maintenance covers.

For resale flats, ask the society for a no-dues certificate and the current monthly maintenance, and check whether there is a transfer fee.

  • Society formation and share money
  • Advance maintenance, often one to two years
  • Parking charges, if any
  • Transfer fee and no-dues certificate for resale

9. Utilities, interiors and moving

Before you move in, you may need to pay deposits for the electricity meter and piped gas, and buy fittings the developer does not provide. Interiors are often the largest cost buyers forget: wardrobes, kitchen fittings, lights, fans and furniture. Add the cost of moving and, for some families, a few months of rent overlapping with the new home.

  • GST at 5%: ₹3,75,000
  • Stamp duty at about 7%: ₹5,25,000
  • Registration: ₹30,000

Ways to keep the total cost down

Some costs are fixed by law, but others can be managed. Compare home loan offers on the total cost, including processing fees and insurance, not just the interest rate. Check whether a woman in the family can be the sole buyer to use the stamp duty concession, keeping in mind its conditions. Ask the developer which charges are negotiable, such as parking or floor-rise, and plan interiors in stages rather than all at once.

Most importantly, decide your total budget first, including every charge in this guide, and then look for flats within it, rather than choosing a flat and discovering the extra costs later.

  • Compare loans on total cost, not only interest rate
  • Check the stamp duty concession for women and its conditions
  • Ask which charges are negotiable
  • Plan interiors in stages

Questions people ask

What is the stamp duty on a flat in Kalyan?
It is commonly about 7% in the Kalyan-Dombivli area, including surcharges. Confirm the exact rate with the sub-registrar or on the IGR Maharashtra website.
Is GST charged on a flat in Kalyan West?
Only on under-construction homes: usually 5%, or 1% for qualifying affordable homes. Ready homes with an occupancy certificate and resale flats have no GST.
How much is the registration fee?
1% of the value, capped at ₹30,000 for properties above ₹30 Lakh.
Do I need to deduct TDS when buying a flat?
Yes, if the price is ₹50 Lakh or more. The buyer deducts 1% and deposits it with the income tax department.
What does an all-in price mean?
It usually means some taxes and charges are already included, but the exact items vary. Ask for a written cost sheet that lists what is included and what is extra.
